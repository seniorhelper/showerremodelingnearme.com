How long does a shower remodel take? 1 day to 3 weeks

It depends on the shower type and the waits nobody can skip.

A shower remodel takes 1 to 2 days for an acrylic or wall panel system once materials are on site, and about 1 to 3 weeks for a custom tile shower. Tile takes longer because of waits you can't rush: Schluter recommends 24 hours before a waterproofing flood test that typically runs another 24, and some grouts need 7 days before water.

In short: the install itself is rarely the slow part. The calendar is set by three things: which shower type you choose, how long materials take to arrive, and what the crew finds behind the old walls. Pick the type first on our shower type comparison, then use the schedules below to plan the days you'll be without a shower.

How long does each type of shower take?

Acrylic, liner and panel systems are made in a factory and cut to fit on site, so most of the time goes to tear-out and plumbing. Solid surface takes a little longer because pieces are heavier and seams are bonded. Tile is built in place, layer by layer, and each layer has to set before the next goes on.

Which waits can't be rushed on a tile shower?

Two of them are set by product instructions, not by the crew:

  • The waterproofing flood test. On Schluter's shower system, the company recommends waiting at least 24 hours after setting its KERDI membrane before water testing, so the thin-set mortar reaches final set, and longer in a cold room. The test itself is "24 hours is typical." That's two days on the calendar before any tile goes up, and it's two days well spent: a leak found now costs a membrane patch, not a tear-out.
  • Grout cure before water. It varies by product. Custom Building Products' Fusion Pro single-component grout (Prism SCG) allows light foot traffic after 24 hours but calls for 7 days at 70°F and 50% relative humidity before intermittent water exposure, and longer when it's colder or damper. Ask which grout your installer is using and read its data sheet; some cure faster, some slower.

When a bid promises a finished, usable tile shower in three days, ask how it handles these two steps.

What adds days before work even starts?

  • Permits. Moving a drain or valve, or changing a tub into a shower, often needs one. How long it takes depends on your city.
  • Materials. Stock acrylic bases and panels may be available quickly; special sizes, colors and custom glass take longer. Order before demolition so the bathroom isn't torn out while you wait.
  • Surprises behind the wall. Old surrounds hide leaks. A good bid says how repairs are handled: a fixed allowance, or priced after demo.
  • Decisions. Valve type, niche location, grab bar backing and glass style all need to be settled before the walls close. Each late decision costs a day.

How do you keep the job on schedule?

  1. Pick the shower type and finish before asking for dates.
  2. Measure the opening and photograph the valve, drain and the floor at the threshold.
  3. Ask every bidder for a written schedule that lists the waits (flood test, grout cure, inspection, glass).
  4. Confirm materials are on site, or ship dates in writing, before demolition.
  5. Plan where you'll shower for the longest case, not the best case.

Shower remodel timing questions

How long does it take to remodel a shower?

About 1 to 2 days for an acrylic or wall panel system once materials are on site, and about 1 to 3 weeks for a custom tile shower. Tile takes longer because of waterproofing, a flood test, tile setting and grout cure. Permits, special orders and damage found behind the old walls add time.

How long after a tile shower is finished can I use it?

Follow the grout maker's data sheet. One common single-component grout, Custom Building Products' Fusion Pro (Prism SCG), calls for 7 days at 70 degrees F and 50% humidity before intermittent water exposure, longer in colder or damper conditions. Other grouts differ, so ask your installer which product they used.

Can a shower be replaced in one day?

Yes, for many acrylic, solid-surface and multi-piece panel systems when the plumbing stays in place and materials are on site. Aging Safely Baths installs its roll-in and walk-in showers in one day in most homes. Tile showers cannot be done in a day because of required set and cure times.

Why does a tile shower take so long?

Each layer needs time: backing and waterproofing, a flood test (Schluter recommends waiting at least 24 hours after setting its KERDI membrane, and a test typically runs 24 hours), then tile, then grout that may need days before it gets wet. Glass is usually measured after the walls are finished.

How long will I be without a shower during a remodel?

Plan on 1 to 2 days with a panel or acrylic system, and on 1 to 3 weeks plus any grout cure period with custom tile. If it is the only shower in the house, line up a second bathroom, a family member's home or a gym before demolition starts.
